# Complete Hook Doctor report

Use the user's language. Ground all observations in the selected video version and state the sampling/time precision. Include a short conclusion first. Use these 13 parts for a full requested report; do not omit sections to save effort.

## 1. Understand the opening

Describe the first frame, person, action, expression, subtitles, large headline, product/results, cuts, camera movement, visual pattern interrupts, and new information in each interval. Distinguish movement observed across frames from a dynamic pose in a still. State whether audio was verified or the analysis relies on visible text.

## 2. Hook Score

Give overall HOOK SCORE XX/100 and six subscores, each with one short evidence-based reason:
- Visual Hook: immediate visual interest and hierarchy.
- Clarity: how quickly topic and viewer benefit become clear.
- Curiosity: a meaningful unresolved question, not just vague withholding.
- Specificity: concrete numbers, outcomes, examples or pain points.
- Scroll-Stopping Power: relevance plus interruption for the intended audience.
- Payoff Speed: time to actual evidence/usefulness, separate from a headline promise.

Use 0–39 for absent/confusing, 40–59 for weak/late, 60–74 for workable with friction, 75–89 for strong/clear, and 90–100 for exceptional alignment. The overall score is editorial judgment, not a measured probability; no false precision or mandatory arithmetic formula.

## 3. Second-by-second analysis

0–1s: What happens? What does the first glance convey? Why stop? Biggest strength/problem?
1–2s: What is newly learned? Is the hook clearer? Does the viewer know what watching will deliver?
2–3s: Has the strongest payoff appeared? Is its timing too late for this structure?
3–5s: Does content progress or repeat? Is there a second reason to stay?

Tie timing to actual media; do not infer spoken onset from a coarse ASR segment boundary.

## 4. First Frame Analysis

Answer: topic evident, clear focal point, distinctiveness, movement in adjacent frames, result-first, curiosity, immediate subtitle readability, and whether a replacement first frame would help. End with First Frame Verdict: Strong / Average / Weak plus reason. A visually attractive frame can still have average communication efficiency.

## 5. Spoken / Text Hook

For first three seconds check: delay to point, filler, excess background, specific outcome, numbers, pain point, contrast, curiosity gap, one removable sentence, and timestamp of strongest line. Quote only verified speech/text and label which. If no language hook exists, say so and analyze the visual equivalent.

## 6. Visual Hook

Assess action/static composition, before/after, final result first, product/result evidence, pattern interrupt, text-image alignment, whether visuals support speech, and meaningful visual change in the first three seconds. Do not mistake two separate examples for a controlled before/after.

## 7. Information Density

List new information at 0–1, 1–2, 2–3 and 3–5 seconds. Mark “資訊推進不足” (or the user's-language equivalent) where there is no material advancement. Decorative motion is not new information.

## 8. Likely Retention Risk

List only defensible risk windows and structural reasons. Explicitly label: “AI 內容結構預測，並非 Instagram 真實略過率／流失數據.” Do not write that viewers actually left at a precise time. User-supplied Insights may be discussed separately as measured data with their actual definitions.

## 9. Biggest Problem

Choose exactly one. Format: 最大 Hook 問題 + one sentence naming the highest-leverage structural issue.

## 10. Keep / Cut / Move Earlier

KEEP: 2–3 strongest elements.
CUT: specific unnecessary phrases/holds/elements.
MOVE EARLIER: actual footage, text or speech and its current timestamp.

## 11. Redesigned 0–3 seconds

Use 0.0–0.8, 0.8–1.8, 1.8–3.0 seconds unless the footage warrants a better rhythm. For each provide 畫面 / 口播 / 字幕, source in/out points where available, layer changes and timing. Distinguish reused speech from required new recording. Check speech duration: do not squeeze an unrealistic sentence into 0.8 seconds.

## 12. Five genuinely different hooks

One each: Result First, Pain Point, Curiosity Gap, Contrarian, Specific Number / Time. Each should be short enough to say in roughly 1–2 seconds when practical, not a caption, with one sentence explaining why it might work. Use only supported numbers and plausible claims; five synonym swaps do not count.

## 13. Final Verdict

Give one answer each:
- If I were the editor, what would I change first?
- What is the single most valuable element to retain?
